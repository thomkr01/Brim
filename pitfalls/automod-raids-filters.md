# Pitfalls: Automod filters

Index and rules: [PITFALLS.md](../PITFALLS.md).

**A short "gg" from seven members made a crowd.**
Every short text's full heat went into the shared crowd pool, so seven members who each typed "gg" within about a second were all deleted, struck and timed out. *Avoid:* a text under 10 characters (after `cure` and the alphanumeric filter) with no link and no mention never joins a crowd pool (`Crowd::MIN_POOL_CHARS`); it still heats its author. Crowd defaults are unchanged.

**An unread level, whitelist or strike row left the scam or spam message posted.**
`check_message` used `?` on the whitelist and level reads (a member GET per tripped message, in the bucket the bans use), and `strike_member` on the strike read and write, all before the delete. *Avoid:* read the level once per message from the message's own member roles (`author_level` through `App::cached_level`; a member fetch only without a member, none for a webhook); the words, links, automod, crowd and trip checks all use it. Moving only the trip read left a member GET per blacklisted link for the whitelist. The level cache also serves `punish_crowd`'s `whitelisted` reads. A failed read deletes the message, punishes nobody and returns the error; `strike_member` deletes the tracked messages when the strike read or write fails.

**The raid ID-farm check was quadratic under the raid lock.**
`flagged` searched a `Vec` of farm ids per joiner and `farm_ids` compared every bucket with every other (about 25 million steps per join for a farm of 5000), under `app.raids`, which every server shares. *Avoid:* `farm_ids` returns a `HashSet` and counts each bucket's margin with sorted buckets and prefix sums. Scanning every bucket when the margin spans more buckets than exist stays quadratic, and nothing caps that (`id_margin_ms` of a year at Hour granularity). Work under a shared lock stays near linear in its input, whatever a setting says.

**A crowd trip deleted the owner's post, and that post counted towards the crowd.**
Every message went into `Crowd::on_text` before the immunity check, so the owner and two members posting one link made a crowd of three; `punish_crowd` deleted every post and only skipped the strike. *Avoid:* immune and `automod`-whitelisted authors never join a crowd (`crowd_exempt` in `check_message`, from the message's own member roles: a member fetch per message would queue behind the punishments in a raid; a failed lookup counts them in).

**One spammer's own heat made the next member who said the same short text a crowd member.**
The crowd pool added every post's heat with no cap per member, and "tripped within the window" took any text. *Avoid:* cap each member's share of a crowd pool at half the max (as `ChannelHeat::shares`); only a long text or link joins a crowd that tripped in the window.

**A trailing dot in a link host skipped every domain check.**
`evil.com.` is `evil.com` to a browser, but missed the phishing, NSFW, blacklisted domain and shortener checks. *Avoid:* `filters::hosts` and `sites::host_of` trim trailing dots; `SiteList::parse` trims them from entries too.

**A downloaded domain list could hold an entry that hits normal chat.**
The phishing and NSFW lists come from a third-party repo with no guard, so an entry like `discord.com` or `co.uk` would act on normal links in every server. *Avoid:* `SiteList::parse` refuses and logs `too_broad` entries: a fixed list of sites chat links to (and their subdomains), bare shared hosting suffixes, and two-label country suffixes.

**The scam-link filter hit Discord's own image links.**
`is_nitro_steam_scam` did not list `discordapp.net` (`media.discordapp.net`), `discord.gift`, `discord.new` or `discordstatus.com` as official, so the host check ("contains discord, not official") flagged them. Each hit is `malicious` (full heat, not skipped by the automod permit) and counts as Raider List scam evidence. *Avoid:* a brand-host check lists every official domain of that brand. The lure words still match inside other words on purpose ("freenitro").

**Spam similarity was slow under the lock every server shares.**
`check_message` holds `app.heat` while `heat` compares a message with up to 5 earlier ones (500x500 Levenshtein each), so a long-message spam raid slowed automod in every server. Moving the comparison out of the lock fails: two messages of one member run in parallel and each must see the other in the history, or a burst of copies is missed. *Avoid:* keep the read, score and remember under one lock; make the score cheap. `best_similarity` returns 1.0 for an identical text and skips a comparison whose length gap already caps it below the best so far.

**A Discord AutoMod rule edited by someone without a permit still drove Brim's punishments.**
Brim runs its own actions (timeout, kick, ban) on hits of a native rule. Anyone with Manage Server can change that rule's keywords, so a member without a permit could make Brim punish innocent messages. *Avoid:* `native::on_rule_audit` reads the audit entry of every rule create or update (live and replayed, anti-nuke on or off). A change by someone who is not Owner, EO, TA or a `dashboard_edit` permit puts the rule in `automod_rule_untrusted` (migration 3925), and `on_native_hit` skips it until a trusted change. A failed permit lookup counts as untrusted; it runs once per entry inside the D20 inflight guard; the newest audit id per rule wins, so a replayed or slower older trusted entry cannot clear a newer untrusted one. Hits between the gateway's rule update and its audit entry wait up to 30 s (`native::rule_changed`). The bot's own entries keep the verdict, since its keyword sync rewrites only part of a rule; a dashboard edit marks the rule trusted itself (`lockdown::trust_rule`). A rollback drops an old `ban_sync` section. The bot's own keyword rule is synced back by `automod::on_rule_change`; no other Discord-side setting feeds a Brim action.

**The audit-log guard for AutoMod rules misses changes it never sees.**
`automod_rule_untrusted` is filled only from audit entries. A bot without View Audit Log, more than 1000 entries while it was down, or a change made before the deploy leaves no verdict, so the rule still drives Brim's actions. *Avoid:* the bot acts only on a rule state a trusted person approved (`automod_rule_approved`, migration 3926). `discord_api::rule_fingerprint` hashes everything that decides hits (lists sorted, missing fields read as empty), so the twilight model and Discord's JSON give one hash (test `twilight_and_discord_json_give_one_fingerprint`). Approvals come from: a dashboard create, edit or switch (`discord_api::approve_rule` in `lockdown.rs`, with the rule Discord returns; a switch approves only if nothing but `enabled` changed since the stored approval, else the Editor is told to review and save it); a trusted actor's audit entry (`native::on_rule_audit` fetches the rule); the bot's keyword sync (actor = bot). The bot keeps each rule's latest fingerprint from gateway create and update events and a fetch of the guild's rules on a miss; a failed fetch takes no action. At GuildCreate, while the guild has no done marker, rules without an approval are approved once (`approved_by` 0, the rule-0 row marks the guild done) unless `automod_rule_untrusted` holds them. A not-approved hit logs once per rule and fingerprint. The audit guard and its 30 s hold stay as a second layer and to name who changed the rule.

**The first-start rule approval ran at the first hit and stopped on a log failure.**
`first_start` ran from the first native hit, so a rule changed between deploy and that hit was approved in its changed state, and a failed approval log (`?`) skipped the guild's done marker, so the next fetch approved again. *Avoid:* `native::on_guild_create` runs the fetch and the one-time approval while the marker row is missing; a hit only fills the cache. Log failures are warnings, never `?` before a marker or a state write (as in `drop_bots`). A guild that adds the bot later, or returns after the purge deleted its marker, gets its rules approved once at that GuildCreate (test `a_guild_back_after_the_purge_gets_its_rules_approved_once`).

**The keyword sync approved a rule it only partly wrote.**
`sync_keyword_rule` sent only the keywords, regex, action and switch, and the bot approved the rule Discord returned, so an exempt role or channel or allow list added in Discord by someone without a permit was kept and approved by the next sync. *Avoid:* the sync owns every field the fingerprint covers: the full `trigger_metadata` (allow list empty), `exempt_roles` from the roles of permits whose whitelist has `words` or `all` (`automod::word_exempt_roles`: only roles that exist and may carry a permit, at most 20), and `exempt_channels` empty. Only then is approving what Discord returns right. `on_rule_change` compares the whole rule (`rule_matches` folds keyword case, then compares fingerprints), so any other change in Discord is synced back. Permit saves write a config version, whose NOTIFY runs the sync. Discord's exempt roles exempt every holder of the role, while Brim's own filter reads only the member's top permit; the local filter still counts every word (`synced_words_still_count_locally`).

**A trusted audit entry approved a later change.**
`on_rule_audit` cleared the hold (`seen_audit`) before it fetched the rule, and approved whatever the fetch returned, also when a newer gateway update had already changed it. *Avoid:* write the approval before `seen_audit`, and only when no gateway update of the rule came more than `LATER_MS` (5 s) after the entry's snowflake time; that later change waits for its own entry. A failed fetch is logged in the AutoMod log and approves nothing.

**The rule cache outlived a new gateway session.**
`RULES` and `FILLED` were filled once per process. A new session (not a resume) can miss rule events, so a held fingerprint could be old. *Avoid:* GuildCreate clears the guild's `FILLED` flag and `RULES` entries (under the `FILL` lock); the next hit or the first-start fetch reads them again.

**Filters only saw plain text.**
Links in `[x](https://..)`, `||spoilers||` or `**bold**`, lookalike letters, zero-width characters, spaced letters (`f u c k`), forwards, polls, user-installed app replies, display names and voice-channel prefixes got past the filters. *Avoid:* filter on `filters::urls` (finds links anywhere) and `filters::word_forms` (cured text). When Discord adds a new place for user text, run it through the instant filters too.

**decancer breaks `String += &String`.**
Its `AddAssign` impl makes `s += &other` ambiguous once decancer is in scope. *Avoid:* use `push_str`.

**Exemptions that were too wide or too narrow.**
Whitelisted channels and roles still got base message heat, other bots' slash-command replies counted as webhook spam, and saying "gg" twice in a quiet channel gave a timeout. *Avoid:* every new heat source checks the whitelist, and quiet-channel heat counts once per user. Add a test with both spammers and normal chatters in one channel.

**A bot rename reads as a new name.**
The post-join username filter dedupes on username + global name + nick. Its Rename action changes the nick, so the echo update looked like a new bad name and the bot would rename on every update; a dehoist nick on a member the filter already punished would punish them again. *Avoid:* for Rename the dedupe key leaves out the nick, and a clean nick counts as done (`joingate::post_join_key`). Auto-dehoist skips a name the filter matched, and its output always starts with a letter or digit, so dehoisting twice changes nothing.

**Personal heat has no ceiling, so a pool can be filled by one member.**
"Heat punishes one member at 100% first" was used to drop auto slowmode's member count. But `HeatState::apply` never caps: one 20-mention message is 315% alone, and the pool is fed before the punishment gates (grace window, automod whitelist, failed timeouts, strikes off). One member could slow a channel, or every channel. *Avoid:* a shared pool caps each member's share at max heat (`ChannelHeat.shares`), and its trigger is above one share (`SLOW_PCT` from 200). The pool starts over when max heat or decay changes, or old heat outweighs the new caps. Before relying on "X stops it first", check that X has a ceiling and runs before the thing it guards.

**Some content arrives after the post.**
Discord adds link and GIF previews in a `MESSAGE_UPDATE` without `edited_timestamp`, and `events.rs` skipped those, so an image filter that only looked at new posts missed every linked image. *Avoid:* a filter on images or embeds also reads preview updates (only that filter, not the whole automod run), and is idempotent per message (a unique key), because the same message arrives again as an update, an edit and through the catch-up sweep.

**Text and images in places the filters did not read.**
The link, invite, scam and word filters read only content, forwarded content and polls, so a webhook or user-installed app could post a phishing link in an embed or link button with empty content. The NSFW scan, image heat and QR check skipped forwarded images, and a user-installed app's reply lost its attachments because it runs as an edit. Thread and reaction names were checked on raw text, and thread renames not at all. Root cause: each filter collected its own input, and a new carrier was added to some only.
*Avoid:* the instant filters read `filter_text` plus `app_text` (embed text and URLs, link buttons and text blocks of webhook and app posts, skipping Discord's previews of links already in the content; a poster's embed is always `rich`, so never taken for a preview); heat still counts only `filter_text`, so a feed's long embed is no spam. Images come from `automod::attachments` (own plus forwarded). Forwards add their `rich` embeds and components (a forward does not say who wrote the original). Files can be new when `automod::files_can_be_new`: a member's post, or any webhook or app message (a deferred app reply too), since those can add files in an edit. A setting that exempts a source from automod (Monitor Webhooks) also stops the image scan, its row and its hit log. Names go through `filters::word_forms` like messages. A thread rename deletes the thread only when the audit log shows its owner renamed it to that name within the last minute (`activity::owner_renamed`); otherwise it is only logged: a `ThreadUpdate` names no actor and also comes for a lock, tags or pins. An edit of a webhook or app message reads only files not seen before (`automod::Seen`, one per check, keyed by (message, attachment) because a forward keeps the attachment ids). A file is marked only once its scan row is saved or its QR image decoded, so a dropped or stopped job is read again. Every webhook or app update (not Brim's own) goes through `on_edit` when its filtered text (content plus `app_text`) or its file list is new for that message (`automod::new_text`, a bounded map keyed by a per-process random hash, since a fixed-key hash can be collided to pass a bad edit), so an image-only fill gets its scan and QR read; otherwise only the preview image scan runs; a LOADING mark misses button updates and races the create. A server-installed app's reply stays exempt from punishment, but its image hit is logged under `interaction_metadata.user`, and the raider hold checks that user too. A message caused by a button click (`automod::clicked`, user- or server-installed app) punishes nobody: the clicker did not write it, so the filters only delete and log it, and the raider hold and the NSFW credit skip it. The NSFW "has new files" check counts only files the scan would queue (`nsfw::scannable`). A flagged rename is logged once per (thread, name).

**An exemption checked after the count, or not on every path.**
Native AutoMod scopes counted a whitelisted member's hits and checked the whitelist only when acting, so their hits filled a server scope for the next member. A bad emoji name in a reaction, and a bad thread name, timed out an automod-permit holder, while the same word in a message is exempt. *Avoid:* check the exemption before anything is counted or deleted, and use the same exemption on every path of one filter (`activity::words_exempt` = words or automod whitelist, as messages get). The mention lockdown counted whitelisted members' pings in full the same way (h4): it now counts only pings the mention, @everyone and automod whitelists do not cover.

**An early return in front of an unrelated protection.**
Heat panic's raider hold sat after "automod off", so with automod off raiders posted freely. The catch channel's test mode returned "handled", so automod skipped every message there. A restore job with no `joined_at` and a `?` on the member lookup gave main roles to a rejoined unverified member, and retried for a member who left. *Avoid:* before an early return, check that everything below it belongs to the switch or case it tests. A delayed role add carries its membership and treats 404 as done (`http_err::member`), and skips a member whose roles are held for verification (`sticky::pending`). A job that gives roles back to a quarantined member adds them to the quarantine's `saved_roles` instead of dropping them, so the release returns them.

**An exemption that covered more than it was for.**
The automod permit (which Auto Setup gives staff) and a webhook whose bot owner is on the anti-nuke whitelist (PluralKit, Tupperbox) returned before every filter, so a hacked mod or a relayed post could send a scam link, an NSFW link or image, or an invite untouched. The catch channel let any permit holder write. Root cause: an exemption meant for spam heat sat in front of the whole punishment path.
*Avoid:* say what an exemption is for and cut there, and check which levels a helper covers: `App::whitelisted` is true for Owner, Extra Owners and Trusted Admins too, and the first fix punished them; they stay exempt (`Level::immune`). A custom permit keeps only the scam, malicious and NSFW parts (`automod::UNEXEMPT`), acted on whatever their heat weight, and its exempt spam takes no punishment claim (the claim comes after the permit check); an exempt webhook still has any instant-filter hit (`INSTANT`) deleted and logged; the catch channel exempts only Owner, Extra Owners and Trusted Admins (`Level::immune`). Native rule hits and reaction/thread names keep the permit exemption (word and spam filters).

**A burst guard that also softened a different punishment.**
The 10 s grace claim after a heat punishment turned a scam or NSFW hit whose filter says Ban into a plain delete, and the crowd's similarity part tied with the scam part and won the "biggest part" pick (`max_by` keeps the last of equal values). Root cause: a guard against punishing one burst twice was applied to every punishment, and a tie went to whatever was pushed last. Letting instant hits skip the window multiplied a scam burst into one warn or ban per message; a per-message "done" marker checked before acting and set after let two passes over one message both punish, and blocked a Ban after a strike on the same message.
*Avoid:* rank punishments (delete < warn < timeout/strike < kick < ban) and, within the window per member (`HeatState::claim_rank`) and per (message, author) (`automod::ACTED`), act only on a stronger one; claim before acting, under the lock, and give the claim back on an error that is not `Punished`, only while the stored claim is still this pass's own (compare and restore), so a stronger claim another pass made meanwhile stays. A tie in `top_part` goes to the first part, and `heat` adds every instant part (blacklisted words too) before the spam parts.

**Handing a filter to Discord dropped what did not fit.**
"Use Discord AutoMod for words" switched the local word filter off for messages, while the synced rule silently left out words over 60 characters, words past 1000 and regex past 10 or 260 characters, and a failed sync was only a trace line. Skipping only the words Discord held also fails open: Discord's rule never sees webhook, app, forward or poll text, exempts admins, can be edited or deleted in Discord, and an in-memory "synced" flag does not survive a restart. *Avoid:* a message that reached the bot was not blocked by Discord, so the local filter always reads every word (with lookalikes); the Discord rule is extra, never a reason to read less. The sync still runs on each save, alerts the automod log when it fails, and syncs the rule back when someone changes or deletes it in Discord (once a minute at most). Thread and reaction names use the same local list, so "Migrate words" only turns the sync on and keeps the lists. Same root cause for reactions and threads: with the auto timeouts off, `activity::heat_up` skipped the strike and the strike-cap lockdown that messages still record.

**Two settings that together meant "do less".**
A filter action Timeout without its own duration hands the length to the strikes; with the strikes' auto timeouts off that became a delete only. *Avoid:* when a setting defers to another system, check what happens when that system is off. `automod::own_action` uses the strike timeout length then.

**Unrolling a short link visited the attacker's server.**
The shortener lookup followed redirects, so the target's server saw the bot's address (not behind Cloudflare), could answer the bot differently from members, and could hold the check for up to 12 s. It also ran only with invites or crawl on, while bad sites and NSFW links read its result. *Avoid:* a HEAD to a known shortener host with redirects off, read `Location` only, and ask again only while it points at another shortener (3 hops at most), within 12 s for the whole message; the final host is never contacted (`Sites::unshorten`). A client that does not build means no unrolling, never a default client that follows redirects. Run it for every filter that reads the target host.

**One shared queue let one server starve the others.**
The NSFW scan's lanes were FIFO across all servers, and a full backlog dropped the oldest job of any server. *Avoid:* a queue shared by servers takes turns per server (`Scanner::take` serves the server served longest ago) and drops or moves from the server with the most jobs (`most_jobs`, for a full backlog and a full live lane alike). The per-member cap (4 live images) was already in.

**Another bot's role was judged per call, and only by its id.**
A bot giving two verification roles in two calls made each look like proof the member was verified already, and a bot role with View Channel on the verified-only channels skipped verification just the same. *Avoid:* note a bot's grants, wait 1.5 s, and judge one member at a time (`verify::given`); take back any added role that opens what a verification role opens and @everyone lacks (View Channel, or Send Messages where @everyone only reads, by base permission or overwrite) or holds Administrator. Check base permissions too, not only overwrites: the usual setup (@everyone without View at server level, the verified role with it) let any "Member" autorole verify. A bot's member overwrite opening such a channel is removed, and GuildCreate reads the audit log for grants made while Brim was down (`given::catch_up`). Every role change used to cost a user and a member read in the member bucket: a per-guild candidate set (60 s), a cached bot flag and a remembered verified member now come first. A cache that skips a security check must be cleared by the events that change its answer, and must not be fed by the actor it guards against. The candidate set is dropped in `invalidate_guild` and on role and channel audit events; a member counts as verified only from a verification role no bot gave just now (`on_overwrite` noted any role, so a bot giving the role and an overwrite together made its later grants skip for 10 minutes), and an un-verify forgets them. `catch_up` reads removals too (Brim's own take-back costs no member read), skips a member that fails, waits out a raid or lock and retries a failed audit read in a heavy job (`verify_catch_up`), and logs when it hits its 500-entry cap. A human was exempt for being human, so Discord's Manage Roles alone could verify anyone; exemptions come only from permits and Owner/EO/TA, so a verification role given by a human without a verify or quarantine permit is taken back too, logged, nobody punished, live and in `catch_up` for grants made while Brim was down. A cache filled by slow reads must not outlive an invalidation that landed during the reads (a per-guild generation in `GATES`; a gate read across a forget is not stored); Brim's own lock edits only shorten the gate to 3 s and its own slowmode keeps it, so a lock over every channel does not rebuild it per joiner, while another actor's edit still drops it at once. A permit-less human cannot give an unverified member a channel-opening role either, and a failed permit lookup takes the role back (fail closed, staff told). `catch_up` retries a run that skipped an actor or member, takes a per-guild lock, runs beside the heavy worker (spawned, like `requarantine_missed`, so neither holds back `panic_start` or `nuke_revert`), and tells staff about its 500-entry cap only after a downtime it may have cut (`bot_seen`, written every minute). A gateway event names no actor, so softening every one let an attacker's edit live 3 s on the old gate; `invalidate_guild` forgets again; only a gateway event about a role or channel Brim marked just before editing it softens (`given::mark_own(gid, id)` in lock, unlock and panic; scoped to the object since a guild-wide mark let anyone open the window by triggering auto slowmode, which needs no mark), and the audit arm drops the gate before anti-nuke runs. A read across a soften is stored with the short life. During a channel lock the gate judges the lock snapshot's saved overwrites (`lockdown::unlocked_channels`), so the Send gate holds while the lock lasts; panic's saved @everyone overwrites are put back the same way (`panic::unexempted`). A self-retrying job counts its failed tries in the payload and gives up after 5 with a staff log; a spawned job's row is finished only on Ok, so the lease and attempt limit apply to errors. A failed lookup or removal is tried once more, then staff hear of it. The hold steps aside only where it acts (a join quarantine, or Quarantine Hold on); a Quarantine Whitelist actor is judged here. Not covered: a channel opened only by two roles together, a member overwrite given while Brim was down, grants older than the newest 500 role changes.

**Image decoding had a per-message limit but no global one.**
The QR check read up to 3 images per message on blocking threads with no cap across messages, so a flood could run hundreds of 128 MB decodes at once. *Avoid:* a global semaphore (`qr::DECODE`, 2 slots) taken after the download (a slow host holds up no other server) and moved into the blocking task. A flood in one server can still slow QR checks in others; that is the heat system's job, and skipping the check when busy would be a bypass. A deleted message must not keep its place in the queue: MessageDelete, bulk deletes and channel or thread deletes mark it (`qr::deleted`, before any await in `events::run`), and no download or decode starts for it; a decode already running finishes and its codes still count, so a scam deleted at once is still acted on. The profile image decode already has a limit; the NSFW scan runs in a fixed set of workers.

**Saving a Discord AutoMod rule turned off all its Brim actions.**
`set_bot_actions` took `&mut config["discord_automod"][key]` for `actions`, `scopes` and `locks`. serde_json's index inserts `null` for a missing key, so a rule with no mention scope or lock saved `"scopes": null, "locks": null`. The bot cannot read a map from `null`, put the whole section back to defaults, and every Brim action on Discord AutoMod rules in that server stopped. *Avoid:* never take `&mut v[key]` on JSON just to read or remove. Use `get_mut` and index only to write a value. The lenient map readers (`lenient::map`, `map_of_lists`) read `null` as empty, so servers that already stored a `null` recover.

- **"Loaded" meant any IP list.** `IpLists::loaded` was true with only the Tor list, so VPN and hosting addresses passed silently. *Avoid:* the refresh warns for each enabled list that is still empty.

- **The NSFW live lane had no per-member cap.** One member's flood filled the shared live lane. *Avoid:* at most 4 images per member wait live (`PER_MEMBER_LIVE`). The rest go to the backlog.

- **A rate gate dropped the last event.** `on_rule_change` ignored a change less than 60 s after the last sync, so a quick second edit in Discord stayed. *Avoid:* a gate that drops events schedules one run at the window's end (`keyword_rule_sync` job, deduplicated per guild).

- **A compare folded more than Discord folds.** `rule_matches` lowercased regex patterns too, but Discord keeps their case, so a changed `(?i)` or letter case was not seen; repeats were not deduped. *Avoid:* fold only what Discord folds (keywords); sort and dedupe lists before comparing.

- **Regex cost had no per-server total.** Each pattern had a size cap, but 50 rules of 10 patterns and 50 words, kept compiled for every server, let one server load the shared bot. *Avoid:* one `RegexSet` per rule with `size_limit` and `dfa_size_limit`, and a server total (30 patterns, 300 words) checked at save under the server lock.

**The domain blocklists held one string per domain and grew with every refresh.**
`SiteList` kept about 1.14 million `String`s in a `HashSet`: about 100 MB, and each 12-hour refresh left freed fragments in glibc's heap (102 -> 178 -> 181 -> 213 MB over refreshes). Even a compact block grew to 110 MB without a trim, because glibc raises its mmap threshold after the first big free and keeps big parse buffers in the heap. *Avoid:* a list of this size is one compact structure (`fst::Set`, built from sorted unique keys on `spawn_blocking`), never a collection of small allocations; a periodic reload that frees big buffers calls `malloc_trim(0)` after the swap (`mem::release_freed_memory`). Not an allocator swap (mimalloc grew more). Numbers: tommybot-data `bot-memory-2026-11-18/methods.md`.

**The image-scan model held 103 MB, most of it freed heap and a second copy of its own bytes.**
Loading the model left ~37 MB of freed buffers in glibc's heap, each scan ~20 MB more, and the 24 MB `include_bytes!` copy stayed resident although tract keeps its own weights. Measured 103 -> 43 MB with both released. *Avoid:* a one-off big allocation (model load, a scan, a list refresh) is followed by `mem::release_freed_memory`; embedded data copied at startup is released with `mem::drop_file_pages` (it discards clean file pages only; a later read reloads them; tests: `mem::tests`, `nsfw::tests::bundled_model_is_the_pinned_one...`). Not an allocator swap (mimalloc 133 MB, jemalloc 98 MB), not unload-when-idle (26 MB more for ~150 ms on the next image), not a quantised or f16 model (8x slower, or does not run). Numbers: tommybot-data `optimise-2026-11-18/results.md`.

**The QR check decoded a reposted image again every time.**
A 12 MP photo costs ~250 ms and ~100 MB of transient memory to decode; a spam flood of the same file paid it per post. *Avoid:* a result cache for pure per-file work is keyed by the SHA-256 of the bytes (never a fast hash: a crafted collision would let a scam image reuse a clean image's result), holds only results that depend on the bytes alone (the QR decode reads nothing from the server's settings, so it is valid for every server), and is bounded in count and age (`qr::CODES`: 2,000 entries, 1 hour, 16 KB of text per entry; tests: `qr::tests`). The NSFW scan results already follow this (`Scanner::cache`).
